**Part 3: Making your agent smarter · Lesson 1 of 4 · 10 minutes**

## Objective

Add the three values that change on every reminder call as custom variables and use them in the prompt, so one agent serves every customer.

## The scenario

In part 2, Ananya said the same thing to everyone. Rahul Verma owes ₹12,450 on 15 October; Priya Sharma owes ₹4,500 on 18 October. The amount, the date and the loan account change per call, so they become variables: the prompt names them once, and each call fills in its own values, from the **Test assistant** dialog now and from a campaign CSV in part 4.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Custom Variables">
    In Ananya's editor sidebar, click **Custom Variables**. The panel lists `callee_name` and `mobile_number`, the two permanent variables, with no remove button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add due_amount">
    Type `Due Amount` into **Enter variable name**. As you type, letters become lowercase and spaces become `_`, so the field reads `due_amount`. Click **Add** or press Enter: "Variables updated successfully". There is no separate Save.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the other two">
    Repeat for `due_date` and `loan_account`. The three new rows sit at the bottom of the list, each with a bin icon.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use them in the prompt">
    Open **Prompt**. In **Conversation Script**, type `@` where a value belongs and pick it under **Custom Variables**; it becomes a chip. Rewrite the steps so they read:

    - Confirm you are speaking to `@{{callee_name}}` about loan account `@{{loan_account}}`.
    - Tell them their EMI of `@{{due_amount}}` is due on `@{{due_date}}`.
    - Ask when they will pay.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cover a missing value">
    A call goes ahead even when a variable has no value. A variable sent empty is left blank; one that is not sent at all stays in the prompt as the literal text `@{{due_date}}`. So add a line to the script: "If the due date is not known, ask the customer to check their statement." Click **Save**: "Changes saved successfully".
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Exercise

Create a variable from the prompt instead of the panel: in any section, type `@{{branch_name` and choose **Create** `"{{branch_name}}"` from the menu. Expected result: a `branch_name` chip in the text and a new row in **Custom Variables**. Then remove the variable with its bin icon: the chip turns red with a wavy underline, because the agent has nothing to fill it with. Delete the chip before you save.

## What you learned

- A variable is written once in the prompt and filled per call: from **Test assistant** now, from a CSV column or the API's `custom_args_values` later.
- Names are lowercase with `_` and unique per agent; the input converts as you type.
- A red token names something the agent does not have. Fix every one before you save.
